7,500+views on one boosted mandi post, then customers walked in holding it up

Hyderabad Peri Peri, Sips and Semkeh, Brooklyn

Social Media Manager and Content Strategist

Three restaurants, three voices: a polished, premium page for Sips, Lebanese street food for Semkeh, and close, raw kitchen footage of biryani, mandi and goat tahari for Hyderabad Peri Peri.

When the owners asked what to boost, I picked a seven-slide post on our goat mandi over a reel. It drew more than 7,500 views and 70 new followers, and ten to fifteen customers came in holding the post up on their phones to order it. The head chef asked what I had posted, because suddenly everyone wanted mandi.

Across about ten weeks I put out 112 posts, the reels passed 45,000 views, and Hyderabad Peri Peri's views rose 139 percent in a month.

Source: Faisal Shahzad, co-owner of Sam & Sha Inc.

Twelve series.
I directed every one.

Canvas is a New York streaming app for micro-dramas and short docuseries, where every episode runs under two minutes and has to end on a hook. I directed twelve of them across New York, Boston, Baltimore, San Francisco and Los Angeles, then stayed through the edit, the grade and the paid launch. A cliffhanger every two minutes is a thumb-stopping ad with a story attached, which is why the strategist in me took the job. Open any series for the story behind it.

The first trailers premiered to about 370 people at the Canvas launch during SF Tech Week, and two days later at House of 434 in Los Angeles.

About, the director's cut

I'm a creative strategist who directs. Or a director who can't stop strategizing. Depends who's buying lunch.

Officially Nivedkrishna Thavarayil, which is exactly why everyone calls me Nived, or Nived Krishna when they're being formal. I've been making things for screens since I was a kid with a YouTube channel and a serious Zach King problem, and selling things since I found out people pay for websites.

All those campaigns taught me one thing: a film, or a brand, works for two reasons. It's made well, and people hear about it. Miss either and it flops. I spent years mastering the second, moved to New York to master the first, and now I do both. That's not a split personality. That's the job.
