I joined W.L.D.D. when it was fifteen people on one floor.

From a top floor in Bangalore to owning ScoopWhoop: what a meme agency's early team taught me.

I joined W.L.D.D. in Bangalore in January 2023, when the whole company fit on the top floor of one building. There were about fifteen of us, and I was one of the first people in that office. I started as a junior brand strategist. Two months later I was a creative strategist, working on campaigns for brands I had grown up with.

Memes were the product

W.L.D.D.'s edge was meme marketing: funny, quirky, shareable copy that people passed on because it was good, not because it was an ad. We did it for Zomato, Swiggy One, Zepto and Bingo! Mad Angles. For films, an organic meme push could put people in seats, and the audience never knew the joke they were sharing had been planned.

The engine was distribution. W.L.D.D. worked with hundreds of India's biggest meme pages, so one idea could land everywhere on the same day. Releases for Prime Video, Netflix India, ZEE5, Sony Pictures, Sony Music and South Indian cinema all ran through that network. By afaqs' count, the agency has worked with more than 120 brands on over 1,050 campaigns, with a community of more than 10,000 creators.

What I made there

I pitched and closed Sony Pictures as W.L.D.D.'s first international client. Our Pope's Exorcist cinema stunt was covered by Hindustan Times, India Today and The Indian Express.

I worked on Thank You Durex, a fan page we built for Durex India. One reel passed 20.9 million views, and I'm the last person in it.

I ran W.L.D.D.'s own Instagram. A typical reel went from about 4,000 views to about 19,000.

I worked on ZEE5's Taj: Divided by Blood, Tu Jhoothi Main Makkaar and Kannada cinema, including PR and marketing around Yash and KGF: Chapter 2.

Then the building filled up

Every few months we needed more room. We took the second floor, then the first, then the whole building. Then came a second building, for LIT School.

I left in 2024, at the peak of my time there, to study filmmaking in New York. W.L.D.D. kept growing. In February 2025 it bought ScoopWhoop from The Good Glamm Group in a deal reported at Rs 20 crore. A month later it took a majority stake in the sneaker brand 7-10, and in June 2025 it acquired the creative studio Imagined.

By July 2026 it had about 300 people across Bengaluru, Mumbai and Delhi. In September 2026, Adfactors PR and Vikas Khemani bought a stake, and Adfactors PR's CEO joined the board.

What I took with me

That floor taught me three things I still use at Thava Studio in New York. Write it so people want to send it to a friend. Put it where the audience already is, not where you wish they were. And ship it while people are still talking about the thing it's about.

Fifteen desks on a top floor became a company of about 300 people that owns ScoopWhoop. I'm glad I was there when it was small enough that every idea I pitched might get made.

When a team is small enough that every idea can ship, speed and taste beat budget.
