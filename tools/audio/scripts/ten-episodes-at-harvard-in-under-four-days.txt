Ten episodes at Harvard in under four days.

And no lead actor on the morning we left.

In August we shot a 10-episode web series at Harvard in under four days, and it turned into one of the wildest shoots I've been part of.

It started with Abhinav Singla, owner of Directure AI, getting a pitch slot at Harvard. He asked if we could turn the whole trip into something more. We said yes before we had a script. The idea was two roommate founders building an AI-powered filmmaking tool, pitching it to investors, and the real Harvard pitch would become the final episode of the series.

We had a baseline idea and three days to shoot it.

The morning we were supposed to leave

Our lead actor stopped answering his phone. We called for hours with nothing back. We pushed our 9am pitch to the afternoon and started driving anyway, hoping he'd still show up. He did, for about twenty minutes, before telling us he had a family emergency and had to turn around and go home.

So now we had no actor, no backup, four hours from New York, and ten episodes still to shoot.

I called every actor I knew in the city. Nobody could clear four days on a few hours' notice. Abhinav ended up calling someone he'd only met once, at a workshop months earlier. Her name is Chewie. She picked up, heard the pitch, and said yes without hesitating for a second. We rewrote her entire role in the car on the drive up.

We missed the actual pitch. We ended up faking it in an empty auditorium the next day, just to get the footage we needed.

Four days, written backwards

What followed was four days of 17-hour shoots, sleeping two hours at a time, and writing each episode based on what we'd already shot the day before, because there was never time to write it first.

Everyone did a bit of everything. Abhinav was acting and producing, Kais Vasaya was shooting as director of photography, and at different points all of us were writing, art directing, or just throwing out ideas because that's what the day needed.

Ten episodes, no real plan, and honestly one of the best things I've made this year.

This is the shoot I think about when people ask how we plan a series so fast. Here's the system we use now, and here's the series page for Demo Day.
