The chain reaction nobody talks about.

Since I started my company in New York, I have brought in more than ten clients entirely through conversation and referral. Not one of them came from a cold database. Every single one came from a chain that started with one person, someone from a film set, a wedding I attended, a real estate contact, a graphic designer I collaborated with once, who knew what I did and connected me to someone who needed it.

This is how it works in practice. You meet someone. You tell them about your work, all of it, not just the part that seems relevant to them in that moment. You ask about their work. You listen. Somewhere in that conversation, a connection forms. Maybe they don't need you right now. But they know someone who does. It's not a system. It's just what happens when you talk to people consistently and honestly about what you do.

Nobody is sitting in a room judging you for talking about your work. Nobody. The only person who thinks it's too much is you.

Your internet presence is your silent pitch. Make it work for you.

Here's something people underestimate: even when you're not in a room with someone, your online presence is pitching on your behalf. Every time a potential client searches your name, visits your Instagram, or reads an article you've written, they're deciding whether to trust you before they've even said hello.

If you don't have a strong portfolio yet, build toward it, not away from it. Show something close to what they need. Show your thinking, your process, your taste. Confidence in the conversation means nothing if there's nothing to back it up when they Google you at midnight before deciding to reply.

And if you genuinely don't have the experience yet, be honest about it and be confident anyway. Every single thing you have done was the first time you did it.
